The Site &
its context

The study area is located in Karşıyaka, on the northern shore of the İzmir Bay. The neighbourhood sits along Cemal Gürsel Bulvarı — one of İzmir's primary coastal arterials — and extends inland through a dense residential fabric dating primarily from the mid-20th century.

The site plan reveals a compact urban block structure punctuated by open green nodes and institutional footprints. The coastal boulevard acts as both a physical and visual edge, separating the built fabric from the İzmir Körfezi and creating a strong east–west orientation that drives prevailing wind and sun patterns across the site.

"The meeting of water, boulevard, and residential mass creates a site rich in tension — between public and private, movement and stillness, hard edge and soft threshold."

The cadastral map identifies distinct zones: a high-density residential core, institutional anchors (Karşıyaka Stadyumu to the east), a network of green circulation corridors, and the transitional zone abutting the coastline. Understanding these layers was the foundation of the analysis.

Analysis Categories

Six lenses,
one territory

🗺️
Land Use
Mapping residential, commercial, institutional, and open-space zones across the site to understand the dominant use patterns and their spatial distribution relative to the coastal edge.
🚶
Circulation
Analysis of pedestrian flow, vehicular access hierarchy, public transport nodes, and the relationship between the coastal boulevard and internal neighbourhood streets.
☀️
Sun & Wind
Solar orientation analysis across seasons combined with prevailing wind direction data from İzmir Körfezi — critical inputs for passive design strategy recommendations.
🌿
Vegetation
Tree canopy coverage, green corridor connectivity, and the ratio of soft to hard landscape surfaces — informing recommendations for thermal comfort and biodiversity.
🏛️
Built Form
Building height, massing, plot coverage, and the relationship of floor-area-ratio to open space — read against the local urban plan (ZMR-1 zone regulations).
👁️
Visual Corridors
Key viewsheds toward İzmir Bay, sight-lines interrupted by building mass, and opportunities to reinforce or create new visual connections with the waterfront.

Land Use Distribution
The site is predominantly residential (68%), with a meaningful proportion of green and open space (15%) — higher than the İzmir average of 9% for comparable urban neighbourhoods. Institutional uses cluster near the eastern edge along the stadium complex.

Circulation Network
Cemal Gürsel Bulvarı carries the primary vehicular load, while a secondary network of 8–12m streets distributes traffic inland. Three roundabouts visible in the cadastral plan act as key nodes. Pedestrian access to the waterfront is limited to four formal crossing points.

Environmental Factors
The site receives strong south-facing solar gain along the coastal edge, with prevailing winds arriving from the northwest off the Aegean. Summer temperatures average 32°C with high humidity. The bay acts as a natural thermal moderator, making passive ventilation strategies highly viable.

Key Findings

What the site
reveals

01
Disconnected Waterfront
Despite the site's proximity to İzmir Bay, only 4 formal pedestrian connections cross Cemal Gürsel Bulvarı. The boulevard functions as a barrier rather than a seam, severing neighbourhood residents from direct waterfront access and reducing use of the coastal public realm.
Priority Intervention
02
Green Space Fragmentation
While the overall green area ratio (15%) appears adequate, open spaces are distributed as isolated pockets rather than a connected network. No continuous green corridor links the residential core to the waterfront — a significant missed opportunity for ecological and pedestrian connectivity.
Urban Ecology
03
Thermal Comfort Potential
The NW prevailing wind from the Aegean, combined with south-facing solar orientation along the coast, creates ideal conditions for cross-ventilation in new and retrofitted buildings. Current building orientations only partially exploit this — most blocks run parallel to, rather than across, the wind path.
Passive Design
04
Stadium as Urban Anchor
Karşıyaka Stadyumu dominates the eastern edge of the site with a large footprint and active transport demand. Its presence creates both opportunity (a major attractor node) and constraint (traffic generation, acoustic impact). A mixed-use buffer zone around the stadium perimeter could mediate these tensions.
